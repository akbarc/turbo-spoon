## Executive Summary

Georgia represents a significant opportunity for convenience store wholesale distributors, with a $297 billion national convenience store market growing at 5.26% CAGR through 2030. The state's 10.7 million population and strategic Southeast location provide access to multi-state distribution networks. Key findings include QuikTrip's market dominance, shifting consumer preferences toward food service over tobacco, and significant private label growth opportunities.

## 1. Georgia Convenience Store Industry Overview

### Market Size and Growth Trends

**National Context:**
- Total U.S. convenience store sales: $297 billion in 2024
- Global convenience store market: $2.39 trillion in 2025, projected to reach $3.11 trillion by 2030
- Growth rate: 5.26% CAGR (2025-2030)
- Over 80% of consumers shop at convenience stores regularly
- Average consumer visits convenience stores 3+ times per week
- 165 million daily convenience store visits nationwide

**Georgia-Specific Metrics:**
- Total consumer spending: $23.81 billion forecast for 2024
- Consumer spending per capita on food/beverages: $1,330 in 2024
- Food market revenue: $3.248 billion in 2024
- Annual growth rate: 7.30%

*Sources: Technavio Market Research, The Business Research Company, Statista*

### Key Competitors and Market Share

**Market Leaders in Georgia:**

1. **QuikTrip (Market Leader)**
   - 122 stores in Georgia vs. RaceTrac's 64 stores
   - Atlanta is QuikTrip's largest market nationwide (139 outlets)
   - Consumer preference leader in convenience store rankings
   - Large-format stores with up to 24 fueling positions

2. **RaceTrac (Primary Competitor)**
   - 64 Georgia locations
   - 75-year presence in Atlanta market
   - Converted to large-format stores in 1999
   - 18th largest private company in U.S. ($19.72 billion revenue)

3. **Circle K**
   - Extensive network presence but specific Georgia store count unavailable
   - Part of national expansion strategy targeting Southeast markets

*Sources: Atlanta Journal-Constitution, Forbes, C-Store Dive*

### Consumer Demographics and Preferences

**Key Demographics:**
- State population: 10.7 million (8th most populous)
- Atlanta metro: 6.3 million (57% of state population)
- Demographics: 33% Black, growing Asian and Hispanic populations
- Younger and multicultural consumers driving category shifts

**2024 Consumer Preferences:**
- **Food Service Overtaking Tobacco:** Food service now largest category
- **Beverage Growth:** Second-highest merchandise sales category
- **Health & Wellness Focus:** 48% of consumers linking mental health to food choices
- **Meal Components:** 60% consider purchasing meals at convenience stores
- **Hydration Trend:** 200% surge in hydration category searches

*Sources: NIQ State of Convenience 2024, U.S. Census Bureau, Convenience Store News*

### Regulatory Environment - Tobacco

**Georgia Tobacco Licensing Requirements (2024-2025):**
- **Licensing Authority:** Georgia Department of Revenue
- **Age Restrictions:** Sale to persons under 21 prohibited for vapor products
- **Vapor Tax Rates:**
  - Closed system: $0.05 per fluid milliliter
  - Open system: 7% of total wholesale cost
- **Distribution Restrictions:** No free distribution within 500 feet of schools/playgrounds
- **License Renewal:** September 2 - December 31 annually

*Sources: Georgia Department of Revenue, Public Health Law Center*

## 2. Wholesale Distribution Opportunities

### B2B Market Dynamics in Georgia

**Market Structure:**
- Georgia serves as Southeast regional hub
- Multi-state distribution territories common
- Next-day delivery standard (orders by 3 PM)
- Full-line, full-service distribution model prevalent

**Key Regional Players:**

1. **BTC Wholesale Distributors**
   - Family-owned, Southeast coverage including Georgia
   - Full-line distributor: tobacco, candy, snacks, grocery, beverages, frozen, dairy, HBC, automotive
   - Expanded from Birmingham AL to five-state operation

2. **Home Folks Wholesale Distributor**
   - 6,000+ products in distribution center
   - Located on GA-SC state line off I-20
   - Next-day delivery service
   - Services GA and SC convenience stores

3. **J.T. Davenport & Sons**
   - 211,000 square foot facility
   - Services 2,000 convenience stores across 8 states including Georgia
   - 340 employees
   - Multi-state coverage: NC, SC, GA, MD, OH, KY, WV, VA

*Sources: Company websites, Trade.gov, MR Checkout*

### Delivery Radius Economics

**Typical Service Models:**
- **Next-Day Delivery:** Standard offering with 3 PM order cutoff
- **Multi-State Territories:** 5-8 state coverage areas common
- **Regional Distribution Strategy:** Warehousing and customer service within designated geographical areas
- **Strategic Locations:** Distribution centers positioned near major interstates (I-20, I-75, I-85)

**Economic Factors:**
- Distribution centers strategically located for multi-state access
- Economies of scale achieved through regional consolidation
- Interstate highway access critical for efficiency

### Category Growth Trends

**Food Service (Fastest Growing):**
- Now largest convenience store category
- 12.2% year-over-year increase
- 60% of consumers consider purchasing meals at c-stores
- 25% regularly visit for lunch

**Packaged Beverages (Second Largest):**
- Hydration category up 200%
- Coffee market: $132.1 billion globally in 2024
- Canned coffee trending among young consumers
- Meal component positioning strategy

**Tobacco (Declining):**
- Only category among top 10 to decline
- All other top 10 categories showed growth
- Regulatory pressures increasing
- Shift toward vapor products with specific tax implications

*Sources: Convenience Store News, NIQ, CSP Daily News*

### Private Label Penetration Rates

**Current Market Status:**
- **U.S. Overall:** 17% private label penetration (vs. 52% Switzerland, 46% UK)
- **Growth Trajectory:** Private label forecast to reach 24% by 2030
- **2023 Performance:** Private label rose 12% to $225 billion
- **Convenience Stores:** 7-Eleven achieved 24% private label unit sales share

**Category Opportunities:**
- **Food & Beverages:** 30% of consumers rate private label quality as superior
- **Growth Potential:** Meat, prepared foods, salty snacks, vegetables, fruit, cheese
- **Wholesale Clubs/C-Stores:** Top channels for private brand growth (90 basis points YoY)

**Quality Perception:**
- 55% of shoppers rank store brands as similar quality to national brands
- Food category shows highest private label acceptance
- Grocery achieves 100% household penetration

*Sources: Food Business News, Private Label Market Research*

## 3. Geographic Expansion Opportunities

### High-Growth Areas in Georgia

**Statewide Growth Indicators:**
- 218 new locations/expansions creating 17,500 jobs
- $13+ billion in investment (first half of FY2023)
- Opportunities "in every zip code of our state" - Gov. Kemp

**Primary Growth Markets:**

1. **Atlanta Metropolitan Area**
   - Population: 6.3+ million (57% of state)
   - 6th highest population increase nationally (2.4% YoY)
   - 51 counties in Georgia, plus AL and NC counties
   - 10th largest U.S. market with 2.5 million TV households

2. **Suburban Growth Centers:**
   - **Sandy Springs:** Home prices up 5.2% YoY, median $650,000
   - **Brookhaven:** Prices up 1.6% YoY, median $665,000
   - **Norcross:** Small-town charm with urban convenience, strong rental demand

3. **Regional Cities (Mixed Performance):**
   - **Augusta-Richmond County:** 206,303 population (declining)
   - **Columbus:** 201,830 population (declining)
   - **Savannah:** 148,808 population (declining)
   - **Athens:** Gaining population

*Sources: Governor's Office, U.S. Census Bureau, Real Estate Market Analysis*

### Underserved Markets/Zip Codes

**Market Gap Opportunities:**
- **Low-income neighborhoods:** Less grocery/convenience access than middle-income areas
- **Predominantly Black neighborhoods:** Reduced retail access compared to white neighborhoods
- **Urban vs. Suburban:** Urban areas have less fresh food retail availability
- **Rural Areas:** 67 of 159 Georgia counties lost population, creating service gaps

**Strategic Considerations:**
- Food desert areas present opportunity for convenience stores
- Federal Opportunity Zones offer tax incentives for investment in low-income communities
- Existing convenience stores may reduce offerings when new competitors enter

**Market Analysis Methodology:**
- ZIP code analysis can identify areas where products are absent despite similar demographics elsewhere
- Color-coded heat maps can highlight zip codes "ripe for exploration"
- Demographics-based targeting for underserved populations

*Sources: County Health Rankings, Georgia Department of Community Affairs, Trade.gov*

### Competition Density Analysis

**Market Concentration:**
- **High Density:** Atlanta metro area with established QuikTrip/RaceTrac competition
- **Medium Density:** Augusta, Columbus, Savannah markets with mixed performance
- **Low Density:** Rural counties experiencing population decline
- **Growth Opportunity:** Suburban Atlanta expansion areas

**Competitive Dynamics:**
- QuikTrip maintains nearly 2:1 advantage over RaceTrac in Georgia
- Large-format stores (20+ fuel positions) dominate high-traffic areas
- Independent operators face pressure from regional chains
- National brands (Circle K, others) expanding into Southeast markets

**Market Entry Considerations:**
- Established players have significant infrastructure advantages
- Regional distributors serve as key suppliers to independent operators
- Private label opportunities exist for differentiation
- Food service focus provides competitive advantage over traditional fuel-focused operators

*Sources: Industry Analysis, Market Research*
